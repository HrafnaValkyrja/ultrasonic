# Plan sanity check (spec v0.4 → v0.5)

**Date:** 2026-09-30 · **Scope:** the whole plan before any schematic or PCB work: physics, chip-level details, parts, power and size, self-noise, bench plan.
**Method:** datasheet and reference-manual checks, a literature review, numeric models (`sim/checks/`), and live JLC stock queries.
**Evidence files:**
- `bone-conduction.md`
- `A1-A2-mcu-and-mic.md`
- `A3-clock-and-peripherals.md`
- `C2-cpu-budget.md`
- `C3-output-stage.md`

## Verdict

**The concept holds.** Nothing found breaks the MVP, and every problem found has a cheap fix. But five things would have caused real trouble if we'd started designing on v0.4, and three of my own earlier claims were wrong. All of it is fixed in spec v0.5.

## 🔴 Must-fix before designing (all applied in v0.5)

| # | Problem | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The two sides would disagree on pitch.** The internal oscillator is ±1–2%. In heterodyne mode, a 1% error shifts the output 380 Hz, so the same bat call comes out at different pitches in each ear. | DS11912 HSI16/MSI tables | Add a crystal: 32.768 kHz (LSE) + MSI PLL mode, ~0.3 µA. An 8 MHz crystal is the fallback, chosen in S1. |
| 2 | **Self-made whine.** The 312.5 kHz PWM isn't a multiple of the sample rate. Any switching coupling into the mic path folds to 87.5 kHz, inside the processed band, and becomes a steady audible tone. | Arithmetic | PWM at **200 kHz**, locked to the same clock as the 200/400 kS/s chain, so coupling folds to 0 Hz and gets filtered out. |
| 3 | **Quiet sounds would be distorted.** Under the planned 3-level (BD) modulation, bridge dead time gives −10 dB THD+N at −40 dBFS even with the shortest possible dead time. | `sim/checks/deadtime_switching.py` | **2-level (AD) modulation.** Dead-time errors cancel whenever ripple current exceeds signal current, which covers quiet listening. Idle cost 0.04–0.4 mW. |
| 4 | **Algorithm B doesn't fit the CPU as written.** Published M4 FFT timings are ~4× my v0.3 guess. STFT + inverse STFT at 200 kS/s needs 85–119% of the CPU. | ARM white paper; measured F446 numbers | Analysis FFT only, with synthesis at the 12.5 kS/s output rate (oscillator bank): 55–81%. Still tight, and to be profiled in S3. |
| 5 | **The mic's response depends on the enclosure.** The datasheet curve (+1.6 dB at 10 kHz, **+14.7 dB at 25 kHz**, ~+8 dB across 35–65 kHz) is an acoustic-path resonance on Knowles' test fixture. Our PCB hole, clasp wall and mesh will change it. | Curve digitized from the datasheet: `sim/data/sph0641_ultrasonic_response.json` | An EQ stage in the DSP; acoustic-path design rules; test "coupons" with the real port geometry. |

Also found and fixed:
- **H-bridge gates float from reset until TIM1 is configured** (RM0394), so the bridge needs gate pull resistors.
- **The DRV8833 bench module can't represent the design.** Its 450 ns dead time, 450 ns deglitch and 1.1 µs delay are 30–40× coarser than ours. It's replaced by a small discrete-bridge test board.
- **DFSDM filter 1 shares a DMA channel with TIM1's update request.** Use filter 0.

## 🟡 My earlier claims that were wrong

- **v0.3: "PWM noise is the biggest technical risk."** Overstated. A 3rd-order shaper puts noise ~90 dB below full scale across 0.2–20 kHz. Bone-conducted ultrasound needs 55–60 dB more drive than the 2–4 kHz band, so the 20–40 kHz worry was overblown. R9 is downgraded; the real output risk was item 3 above.
- **v0.4: "the Elecrow mic breakout is unverified."** It exists and is in stock ($12.50). v0.5 buys it for S1 and keeps custom boards for acoustic-path tests only.
- **v0.3: "plan for ~100 mAh for all-day wear."** A 100 mAh cell is 30×12×4 mm and 3 g, bigger than the whole PCB. That fights priority #1 (comfort and size). The MVP only needs 2+ hours. This is now a decision for the owner (below).

## 🟢 Confirmed, with new knowledge

- **Transducer site (D1):** the literature backs the cheekbone-arch/condyle region over the temple by 3–14 dB. **Moving closer to the tragus could add ~10 dB of loudness and much better left/right isolation** (sound radiated into the ear canal via cartilage), if the Ear Open pod leaves room. E2 now tests that spot.
- **Stereo (T3):** bone-conduction stereo localizes about as well as headphones when phase-coherent (~22–23° error vs ~20°). Our unlinked units have random relative phase. At the ~10–15 dB isolation expected at D1's site, that makes the left/right *size* vary, but a simple model keeps its *direction*, which is what T3 needs.
  - S4 adds an A/B test: free-running units vs units sharing one clock. It measures whether a link between sides would be worth adding.
  - Keep the output band ≥1.5 kHz, where the ear's phase sensitivity fades.
- **Contact force:** aim for ≥1 N through a compliant pad. Our inertial transducer loses only a few dB at ~1 N, and more, less repeatably, below 0.5 N.
- **Mic lifecycle:** Active at DigiKey (22k in stock). Syntiant reissued the datasheet in Dec 2024. JLC stocks 1,076. Backup: TDK T5838, but it's only characterized to 50 kHz (covers bats).
- **Pin map:** every function needed is available on the 48-pin L452 (mic clock PA5, data PB1, bridge PA8/PA7/PA9/PB0).
- **MVP band edge:** the mic is characterized to 80 kHz and the 200 kS/s chain passes ~80–85 kHz. That's within "roughly 20–96 kHz", but the top ~10 kHz isn't realistically there. An option exists (4.44 MHz mic clock → 247 kS/s, +23% CPU) if the top octave matters to you.

## Open items to measure, not decide

- Mic clock duty cycle: DFSDM is characterized at 45–55% on a sister part, and the mic needs 48–52%. Scope it in S1; there's a fallback.
- Transducer coil inductance (E1). It sets the clean range of AD modulation and may move the PWM to 100 kHz.
- RC-BC02 impedance: the vendor's own pages say 8 Ω in one place and 12 Ω in another. Measure it.
- Self-noise the device will hear: your own speech sibilants, chewing, hair on the clasp, frame creaks. Add these to the S1 recording checklist.
