## Tools
- **Soldering:**
  - temperature-controlled iron at about 330 °C (the LED datasheet allows 350 °C for 3 s by hand);
  - 0.2–0.5 mm conical or chisel tip;
  - flux pen, 0.3 mm solder, desoldering wick.
- **ESD:** wrist strap or grounded mat. **The LED survives only 150 V HBM.**
- **Handling:** fine tweezers (one pair insulated, for the cell's tabs), a 5–10× loupe, a scalpel, flat-nose pliers, 400-grit paper on a flat block, calipers.
- **Testing:** a multimeter with a continuity beeper; a bench supply with a current limit, or the cell itself.
- **Programming:** an ST-Link V2 (a clone is fine), Dupont leads, and later the 1.27 mm pogo probe.

## Tolerances you check yourself
- **Pod board height** after depanelling: 11.5 mm. The lid's webs sit 0.2 mm beyond each edge, so **sand the mouse-bite nubs to ≤ 0.1 mm proud.**
- **Pod board front edge:**
  - The tub's front stops touch it only in the 0.6 mm bands, and the front wall is 0.3 mm away.
  - A leftover SWD-tab stub fits, but **sand it flush anyway.**
- **Pad board:** 5.0 × 9.3 +0 / −0.1 mm after sanding its two nubs. The cap recess allows 0.15 per side.
- **Pad board stack:** the LED and the wire joints must stay **≤ 0.45 mm proud** of the board, so the parts top is ≤ y 3.9. The cap's joint pockets are sized for exactly that, so flatten big solder blobs.
- **Pod board joints:** ≤ 0.45 mm proud on the outer face. The lid's parts envelope allows 1.2 mm, so this is easy.

## Assembly order

**A. Bench bring-up, boards still in the panel** (rev 1 is the prototype; the test strip is the debug access, spec O9)
1. **Inspect the panel.** Look for the cathode mark on each LED and on the 0402s near the rear pads.
2. **Solder the 1×5 header** onto the test strip by each pod board. Pins: 3V0, SWDIO, GND, SWCLK, NRST.
3. **Power the board.** Clip a bench supply at 3.7 V, 50 mA limit, onto BAT+ and BAT−. JLC pre-tinned the pads, so a short tinned lead tacks on with one touch.
4. **Flash** through the header.
   - ST-Link SWDIO/SWCLK/GND/NRST to the header.
   - **3V0 is a sense pin only.** Use it with a genuine ST-LINK; on a clone leave it unconnected.
   - **Never connect a clone's 3.3 V output.**
5. **Bench tests.**
   - **Bridge:** clip an 8–12 Ω resistor across OUT_A/OUT_B.
   - **Charger:** 5 V (current-limited) on VBUS and GND_CHG, with a cell on BAT±.
   - **LED drive:** tack a pad board to LED+/LED− with two short wires.
6. **Remove every temporary lead** with wick.

**B. Depanel**

7. **The SWD tab** (front edge of each pod board): score both faces on the board edge line with a scalpel, then snap it with flat pliers on the strip side.
8. **The mouse bites:** snap the pod boards and pad boards out.
9. **Sand** the top and bottom edges and the front edge flush; check with calipers.
10. **Seal the 5 cut trace ends** on the front edge with a dot of nail varnish or UV solder mask.
11. **Meter check:** 3V0 to GND must not read a short.

**C. Pad** (mechanical steps in pad.md; these are the electrical ones)

12. **Wires through the cap first** (pad.md step 2). The four arm wires go through the cap's channel before you solder them, because they can't be threaded afterwards.
13. **Solder, wearing the wrist strap.**
    - The four arm wires go to the top row by silk label: J1 OUT_A, J2 OUT_B, J3 "+", J4 "−".
    - The transducer leads come up through J5 (XDCR_A) and J6 (XDCR_B) from below; trim them flush on top.
    - The left pad carries the same board turned over, so its labels sit mirrored. **Follow the labels, not the positions.**
14. **Meter-check** the four arm pads for bridges; they are 0.45 mm apart. **Don't coat the joints with RTV:** the cap's pockets are sized for bare 0.45 mm joints.
15. **LED test:** 3–4 V through ~2 kΩ, + to LED+. It should glow blue at 0.3–0.6 mA. If it stays dark, it is almost certainly reversed. A reversed LED isn't damaged: 4 V is under its 5 V reverse limit.
16. **Close the pad and cast the ring** (pad.md steps 4–8).

**D. Pod** (the shell's order; electrical points in bold)

17. **Charge nuts:**
    - Solder a 35 mm wire to each brass nut on the bench (resin softens at 60–80 °C).
    - Press the **VBUS nut up into the upper boss** and the **GND_CHG nut down into the lower boss**.
    - **Seal each slot mouth with a dot of RTV.** The arm wires pass right there, and the VBUS nut is live.
18. **Cell, then the foam strips** (shell.md steps 3–4).
    - **The PCM's tabs are a short-circuit risk:** use insulated tweezers, one tab at a time.
19. **Solder the pod-board wires on the bench,** in the agreed pad order (proposed, top to bottom: VBUS, GND_CHG, BAT−, OUT_A, OUT_B, LED−, LED+, BAT+).
    - Solder all except **BAT+**.
    - **Meter-check** every neighbouring pair of pads for a bridge.
    - **Then solder BAT+ last.**
    - Leave ~35 mm of free length on every wire, so the board can lie beside the pod.
20. **Board in:** lay it on the foam strips, outer face (MCU side) out, mic end forward, about 0.5 mm back from the front stops. Fold the wire loop into the rear space (x 50.6–62); it fills about 15 % of it.
21. **Mic washer, lid, screws** (shell.md steps 6–8).
    - Lower the lid 0.45 mm behind its seat, push it forward, and fit both screws **snug only**. They are now the charge contacts.
    - **Don't paint the screw heads.**
22. **Test:**
    - Continuity from the top screw head to the VBUS pad, and from the bottom head to GND_CHG.
    - Then 5 V through a 100 mA current limit on the heads (top = +). The supply should show the charge current.

## Disassembly order (reflash, repair)

1. **Undock.** Slide the pod forward off the frame adapter (shell.md).
2. **Lid off.**
   - Take out both screws: the charge contacts leave with them.
   - Pull the lid back 0.45 mm and lift it.
   - Keep the mic-seal washer.
3. **Reflash.**
   - Lift the board out by its edges and lay it **outer face up on the bench** beside the pod, on its wires.
   - Hold the 5-pin pogo probe on the SWD column at the board's front edge (§7 of the research note). Power comes from the cell.
   - Don't probe the board while it sits on its foam: it tilts.
4. **To free the board:** **desolder BAT+ first**, then the others as needed.
5. **Pad:** open it as in pad.md's disassembly order. The board lifts out with the transducer; desolder only what you're swapping.
6. **Nuts:** peel the RTV and slide them out with tweezers. Desolder their wires at the pod board's end.

## Electrical rules this build relies on (checked)

**The exposed screws are safe while worn.**
- Undocked, the +5 V screw sits at ≤ 0.4 V: the charger leaks ≤ 2 µA back (MCP73831/2 datasheet DS20001984H) into a 200 kΩ divider.
- Sweat across the screws does nothing while worn: the heads are 8.3 mm apart and neither side is driven.
- **Wipe the pod before docking.** Docked and wet, the +5 V screw corrodes.

**ESD on the +5 V screw is handled.**
- D3 is rated ±30 kV (onsemi ESD9X, Rev 7, 2012).
- C15 soaks up an 8 kV hit with a 0.26 V rise.
- **Place D3 right at the VBUS pad.**

**Upside-down on the dock reverses the polarity. Not handled yet.**
- Today that can kill D3.
- **Proposed fix:** a 1N5819WS series Schottky. It's a small 40 V diode: C191023, Basic, 4,795,058 in stock, $0.0137 (JLC parts API, 2026-10-01T00:01Z).
- Until that's in, **only dock the glasses upright**, and use a current-limited 5 V supply.

**The LED wire is unprotected for now.**
- **Proposed:** split R14 into 1k + 1k (C11702, Basic), so a damaged arm wire can't overload PB7.
- Until then, **check the arm wires for nicks before sleeving.**

## Open risks (blunt)
1. **The board mounting depends on the shell's proposal, which the owner hasn't approved** (USER_REVIEW).
   - The frame's own plan (tub ledges, screws at x 60.8) can't take the cell, per shell.md.
   - Nothing on the board changes either way: both plans only touch the 0.6 mm bands.
2. **Left/right:** the frame is the right pod, while the only drawn layout (`hw/pod/place.py`) is the left pod's board.
   - One board serves both pods only if the mic port and the button sit on the board's centre line (option L3), or the two shells differ (L2). **Owner decision.**
3. **The button (x 42.5, z 1.9) overlaps a centred MCU by 0.6 mm.** The layout can't keep both as drawn.
4. **The frame's wire pads** are 0.3 mm apart and 0.2 mm from the edge. The top one, if z_top is a pad centre, sits 0.35 mm into the clamp band. The proposed staggered 1.0 × 1.6 pads need 3.4 mm of the outer face, not 1.2.
5. **The frame's pad order puts BAT+ next to BAT−:** one bridge shorts the cell. A reorder is proposed.
6. **`frame.PAD_Y["led_top"]` is 3.75, but the LED top is at 3.88.** pad.py already builds to 3.88.
7. **The transducer's leads are unknown** (E1). The pad board has 0.45 mm holes at (±1.775, 3.275), with lap-soldering as the fallback.
8. **pad.py still describes the old J5/J6 position** in `PADBOARD_PROPOSED` ((±1.85, 3.25), Ø0.9). Mine passes its own joint check (checks.json), but its file should match.
9. **The spec still says magnetic pogo charging** (§3, §8, B6). Screw contacts need the owner's OK and a spec edit.
10. **JLC charges a different-design fee** for pod + pad board in one panel. The amount appears only in the quote.
11. **The pad-board layout is a placement draft by script** (DRC 0, ERC 0). The owner may redraw it. The pod board is still the owner's layout to do.
